# 06 · Case Study — MapleStory N

**Scale:** dozens of accounts · **Result:** ≈ ¥1.02M realized · **Period:** 2024–2025

## Setup

A beloved legacy IP brought on-chain, with a large nostalgic player base and an item economy with real external demand. Fewer accounts than the Pirate Nation campaign — and more profit. The difference was selection and pricing discipline, not scale.

## What decided it

- **IP gravity is a market signal.** A famous IP brings genuine players, and genuine players bring durable demand for items and progression. Campaign selection weighted that heavily (doc 02, stage 1).
- **Item-market operations.** Revenue came primarily from playing the item economy well — acquiring underpriced, supplying into event-driven demand spikes — with rewards as a bonus, not the thesis.
- **Smaller matrix, deeper play.** Dozens of well-run accounts outperformed hundreds of shallow ones. Depth per account raised both survival and per-account net (doc 04).

## Lessons carried forward

1. Campaign selection is the highest-leverage decision in the whole workflow; a great operation on a dead game still dies.
2. Per-account depth beats raw account count once a market has real players in it.
3. Across both campaigns: ≈ ¥1.62M realized came from process — cohorts, ledgers, exit rules — applied consistently, not from any single lucky drop.
